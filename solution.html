How can it improve
citizen health?

Our smart smellscape solution improves health by detecting pollution, bad smells, waste, and drainage issues early.

Physical Health
Our solution can improve physical health by reducing exposure to bad smells, smoke, dust, harmful gases, sewage odors, and waste pollution. Cleaner air and better waste control can help protect people from breathing discomfort and unhygienic environments.
Mental Health
Cleaner and fresher-smelling areas can make people feel more comfortable, calm, and relaxed. Reducing unpleasant smells from garbage, drains, and traffic can lower stress, annoyance, and discomfort in daily city life.
Social Well-being
When streets, markets, parks, and public spaces smell cleaner, people are more likely to walk, relax, meet others, and enjoy the city. This improves community comfort and makes urban spaces more welcoming.

All The Sensors Usage

VOC sensor
Detects volatile organic compounds, monitoring chemical emissions in real-time. When VOC gases touch the sensor, electrical resistance changes and is converted into data, helping the system recognize general odor patterns such as food or waste.
NO2 sensor
Measures nitrogen dioxide concentrations primarily emitted from high-density vehicle exhaust and converts into data to show the levels of NO2 present.
CO sensor
Detects carbon monoxide, works by allowing air to enter the sensor, then reacting with carbon monoxide particle and converts into electrical signals, indicating stronger electrical signals as higher concentrations of CO.
Ammonia sensor
An ammonia sensor detects NH₃, a gas often linked to urine-smells and decomposing wasteworks works by reacting with ammonia molecules and producing signals that show the concentration level with higher ammonia levels meaning stronger smells.
Hydrogen sulfide sensor
A hydrogen sulfide sensor detects H₂S, a gas that smells like rotten eggs, often produced by sewage and decomposing organic matter. It works by reacting with hydrogen sulfide gas and turning that reaction into an electrical signal to detect sewage-like smells early.
PM2.5 sensor
Tracks microscopic fine particulate matter to calculate instant air quality and smog levels.
Humidity sensor
Measures atmospheric moisture content to predict dense fog, rainfall probability, and condensation risks.
Wind sensor
Monitors shifting wind velocities and air current directions to map out urban emission scatter paths.
Temperature sensor
Tracks thermal shifts and urban heat island fluctuations to monitor environmental changes in real-time through changes in electrical resistance.
PM10 sensor
Monitors larger airborne particulate matters like dust, pollen, and wild road debris vectors.
Methane sensor
A methane sensor detects CH₄, (a gas produced when organic waste decomposes) by sensing methane molecules in the air and some use heated sensor materials that change their electrical resistance when methane is present with data of methane level.
Smart bin sensor
A smart bin sensor detects how full a garbage bin is using an ultrasonic sensor placed inside by measuring the time it takes for the reflected sound waves to return for the system to calculate how full the bin is.
Water level sensor
A water level sensor measures how height of water and uses ultrasonic waves or pressure sensors. If water level is too high or flow is too slow, the system can detect possible blockage or stagnant water.
